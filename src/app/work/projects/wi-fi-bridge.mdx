# Overview

Imagine managing padlocks in remote locations—warehouses, construction sites, or rental properties—where security is critical, but access is a logistical nightmare. You’re a facilities manager overseeing a remote warehouse. A contractor needs temporary access, but you’re miles away, and driving out to update a lock code isn’t an option. This was the reality for many Master Lock users—until we designed the Wi-Fi Bridge.

## The Challenge

Our team identified several key issues through research and customer feedback:

1. **Remote Access Struggles**: Warehouses, construction sites, and rental properties often lack supervised access management, relying on manual processes that compromise security and efficiency.
2. **Bluetooth Limitations**: While Bluetooth padlocks offered some advantages via the Vault Enterprise app, they lacked remote access and real-time notifications, requiring users to be within close range to manage them.
3. **Battery Life Concerns**: Adding Wi-Fi directly to padlocks was impractical—locks in remote locations like telecommunication towers might sit unused for months, and constant connectivity would drain their batteries.

One user summed it up: _“I spend hours driving to sites just to change a lock code. It’s inefficient and costly.”_ 

Our challenge was clear: design a solution that delivers remote functionality without undermining the padlock’s core purpose.

---

## Research and Discovery

To address these challenges, I collaborated with product management, and external agency teams on a comprehensive research effort:

- **User Interviews**: Our agency partner interviewed current Master Lock Bluetooth device owners, revealing that 70% struggled with remote access management.
- **Internal Insights**: Feedback from sales and channel marketing highlighted business needs and user expectations.
- **Competitor Analysis**: We studied similar products to identify opportunities for differentiation.

![Research findings.](/images/projects/wifi-bridge/research.jpg)

---

From this, we crafted four personas to guide our design:

1. **The Decision Maker**: Owners seeking to upgrade access control.
2. **The Operator**: Employees using locks to enter restricted areas.
3. **The Coordinator**: Facilities managers overseeing access and personnel.
4. **The Provisioner**: Staff responsible for setting up the bridge and connecting locks.

These personas shaped our approach. For instance, the Provisioner needed an intuitive onboarding process, which became a cornerstone of our design.

## Ideation and Conceptualization

I kicked things off by leading weekly working sessions with design, product, and engineering teams. These collaborative meetings were crucial for aligning everyone on user needs and brainstorming solutions. We mapped out user flows, debated trade-offs, and prioritized features, ensuring our designs were both user-friendly and technically feasible. These sessions set the foundation for the innovative solutions that followed.

### Mapping the User Journey

I led weekly UX workshops to map out critical user flows, including:
- Provisioning the bridge and adding it to the system.
- Connecting the bridge to Wi-Fi.
- Managing padlocks and access.
- Receiving real-time notifications.
- Troubleshooting network issues.

These flows became the backbone of our high-fidelity wireframes and prototypes, which I designed for both mobile and web platforms.

![User flows for different features.](/images/projects/wifi-bridge/user-journey-mapping.jpg)

## Design Process: Crafting the Experience

I created wireframes and prototypes for both mobile and web applicaitons, including the interface design and user flow with details for developers.
I tested the prototypes with users and iterated on the design based on feedback, then presented those findings and designs to product and software management. Once apporved I supported developers throught the whole process.
<br />

### Adding a Bridge

Connecting the bridge to the local network has to be seamless. Access to a Wi-Fi network has to happen automatically or as seamlessly as possible with all the locks on the account.

![Wireflow describing how to add bridge to the Enterprise app.](/images/projects/wifi-bridge/add-bridge-mobile.jpg)

### Online Status

For effective performance, the padlock must be within an optimal distance from the bridge; if it is not, we need to notify the user immediately.

![Wireflow describing how to check bridge wifi status.](/images/projects/wifi-bridge/wifi-status.jpg)

### Bridge Details

We should include only bridge details necessary for optimal functions and lock access.

### Events History

For monitoring device health and Wi-Fi strength through time.

### Find, Delete or Re-Activate

For the use cases where owner is oftentimes changing bridge location, such as realtors when selling different properties.

## Impact and Reflections

Though the project didn’t launch due to the changing priorities, it earned praise from stakeholders. One product manager remarked, _“This is the most user-friendly interface we’ve seen for a complex product like this.”_ The experience taught me valuable lessons:
- **Iterative Design**: Regular testing uncovered and resolved usability issues early.

- **Strategic Thinking**: Updating the design system added long-term value beyond the immediate project.
