# Overview

The Master Lock Wi-Fi bridge enables remote access and real-time notifications for Bluetooth-enabled padlocks without sacrificing battery life. It will allow users to manage access and receive notifications from anywhere while enhancing the functionality of our whole product line and opening new markets for the business.

## The Problem

Through research and customer feedback, we have identified several key issues:
1. Remote locations, such as warehouses, construction sites, and rental properties, encounter challenges with access management. These areas are often unsupervised and managed manually, leading to security concerns.
2. While Bluetooth padlocks can help address these security issues—mainly when accessed through the Vault Enterprise mobile app—they do not offer real-time notifications or remote access capabilities.
3. Moreover, the Bluetooth range is limited, and users must manually wake up the lock, making current solutions inadequate for remote access or password updates.
4. Battery life is critical because locks may be used infrequently in remote areas (such as telecommunication towers). Adding Wi-Fi connectivity to those locks could quickly drain their battery life and defeat their primary purpose.

## Solution

![Finding the bridge in the desktop app.](/images/projects/wifi-bridge/finding-bridge-web.mp4)
Finding the bridge in the desktop app.

The Master Lock Wi-Fi bridge addresses all issues by enabling remote access, updates, and real-time notifications for Bluetooth padlocks without sacrificing battery life.

Key features this product will have to satisfy business requirements, and user needs:
1. **Adding a Bridge**: Connecting the bridge to the local network has to be seamless. Access to a Wi-Fi network has to happen automatically or as seamlessly as possible with all the locks on the account.
2. **Online Status**: For effective performance, the padlock must be within an optimal distance from the bridge; if it is not, we need to notify the user immediately.
3. **Bridge Details**: We should include only bridge details necessary for optimal functions and lock access.
4. **Events History**: For monitoring device health and Wi-Fi strength through time.
5. **Find, Delete or Re-Activate**: For the use cases where owner is oftentimes changing bridge location, such as realtors when selling different properties.

## Research

## User Personas

After the field research and conversations with our sales and channel teams we created four personas based on their role in the process:
1. **The Decision Maker**: usually the owner who decides to enhance the access control system.
2. **The Operator**: an employee who uses locks to enter restricted areas.
3. **The Coordinator**: The facilities manager is in charge of personnel and access.
4. **The Provisioner**: an employee that will set the bridge up and connect individual locks to Wi-Fi.

## User Flows

I led UX workshops where we mapped user flows from initial bridge setup, troubleshooting, connection to Wi-Fi network and more.
Key touchpoints in the journey include:
1. Provisioning and adding bridge to the system
2. Setting up the product and connecting it to Wi-Fi
3. Adding and managing padlocks
4. Receiving real-time notifications and alerts
5. Troubleshooting, errors and changing networks

## Conclusion

The Master Lock Wi-Fi Bridge project has successfully tackled the challenges of remote access and management for Bluetooth-enabled padlocks by introducing seamless integration, real-time notifications, and functionality enhancements without compromising battery life. While not reaching the launch stage, the project has been an invaluable learning experience that has equipped us with key insights into user needs and product requirements. Our research-driven approach, focused on through well-defined personas, requirements and continuous testing, resulted in a design that is intuitive and robust and ready to be implemented in the future.
