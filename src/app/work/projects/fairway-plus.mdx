## Overview

Fairway+ is an AI associate assistant for in-store retail, built at Epicor with Ace Hardware as lead customer. A floor associate gets stopped mid-aisle with a half-remembered product question, and needs an answer — price, aisle, availability — before the customer walks away.

The assistant inside the app is **Prism**, Epicor’s proprietary AI. The two names are deliberate: Fairway+ is the product an associate installs and logs into; Prism is the intelligence they talk to, and it also powers other Epicor surfaces.

I owned UX and UI end to end: the conversational model, the answer hierarchy inside a chat bubble, disambiguation, comparison in-thread, follow-up for related SKUs, and the detail surface underneath it.

## The problem

Three constraints from the floor, not the mock:

1. **The question is vague by nature.** Associates repeat what a customer said, not what a catalog indexes: “the black deck screws, the long ones.” A search box punishes that. A conversation absorbs it.
2. **The environment is hostile to reading.** Noise, one free hand, a customer standing there waiting. Any answer that requires scanning a results page has already lost. The first line has to carry the payload.
3. **Wrong answers cost more than slow ones.** Sending someone to the wrong aisle burns trust twice — with the customer and with the associate who stops using the tool. Ambiguity had to be surfaced, not silently resolved.

---

## Decisions

### Chat as the operating system

Every task — lookup, compare, stock check, detail — resolves in one thread instead of separate tabs. One place to return to, one history to scroll, no mode switching while holding a box of screws.

### Answer in the bubble

The response leads with the two facts an associate is actually being asked for: aisle and price. Specs, alternates, and imagery sit below, reachable but never in the way of the answer.

### Disambiguate, don’t dump

When a query maps to multiple SKUs, Prism asks one clarifying question with a small set of distinguishable options. A 40-row result set is a failure state disguised as thoroughness.

### Compare inside the thread

Comparison renders as a compact table in the conversation, scoped to the attributes that differ. The associate keeps their place and can keep talking to it.

### Deep detail when needed

A full product surface — carousel, specs, aisle — is one tap from the bubble. Depth exists for the 20% of questions that need it.

## Home

A splash that names the assistant, then a greeting and a single input. Home is not a dashboard — it is a prompt with as little standing between the associate and their first question as possible.

The splash reads “Welcome to Prism” by design, so the associate learns the name of the thing answering them — not the name of the icon they tapped.

## Outcome

- **Shipped** on Google Play and the App Store as Epicor Fairway+.
- **Lead customer:** Ace Hardware, onboarded with access to the shipped build.
- **Distribution** is enterprise and limited — a public Play listing with 1+ downloads is expected for a gated rollout, not a usage signal.
- **Adoption** data is still pending. Store and associate usage counts are not yet reported.

---

## Reflections

**The naming question was a systems question.** Two names in one app reads as a bug until you see the surface map. Prism is a platform capability; Fairway+ is one of its clients. I designed the moment of introduction so the distinction teaches itself rather than arguing for a rename that would have cost Epicor its cross-product identity.

**I designed the answer, not the screen.** Most of the work was deciding what belongs in the first two lines of a bubble. Once aisle-then-price was settled, layout, disambiguation, and the detail layer all fell out of it.

**What I would pressure-test next.** Adoption data is still pending, so the honest open question is whether associates return to the thread after their first answer. That is the metric I would instrument first: repeat questions per shift, not downloads.
